How the verdict works

The calculator divides the restaurant price by the shop price of the same bottle (same producer, vintage and 750 ml size):

  • 1.75× or less: great value. Clearly below the usual US restaurant markup of about twice the shop price, and better than even the best-value UK lists (about 1.8–2×).
  • 1.75× to 3×: fair price. The span from a typical US list to the UK average of about three times retail.
  • 3× or more: high markup. Above what most restaurants charge.

Tips for an honest comparison

  • Use a normal local shop price, not the cheapest price you can find online. The cheapest online price makes every markup look bigger.
  • By the glass: pick the pour size from the menu. The calculator converts the glass price to a bottle price (a 750 ml bottle holds five 150 ml glasses). In the US, glasses often come out as a high markup: a common rule prices one glass at what the restaurant paid for the whole bottle.
  • In Sweden, comparing with the Systembolaget price is the natural check, but Systembolaget's price is close to what restaurants themselves pay. A wine at 3× its Systembolaget price is a steeper markup than 3× a US shop price.
  • Expensive bottles usually get a lower multiple than cheap ones, so a fair price on a 150-dollar bottle often sits below 2×.

Frequently asked questions

What is a normal markup on restaurant wine?

About twice the shop price in the US (three times what the restaurant paid) and about three times retail in the UK. Sources and details.

Does the calculator store what I type?

No. It runs entirely in your browser and sends nothing anywhere. A result only becomes a link if you choose to copy one.

Which currency does it use?

Any. Enter both prices in the same currency; the markup is the same either way.
