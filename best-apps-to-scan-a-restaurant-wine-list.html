Best apps to scan a restaurant wine list (2026), compared

Checked 17 November 2026 · By the PocSomm team

We make PocSomm, so we've put it first. Everything about the other apps comes from their own App Store listings and websites, checked on 17 November 2026; prices are US App Store prices. Spotted something out of date? Tell us at support@pocsomm.com.

For most diners, PocSomm is the best all-round choice. It prices every bottle on the list, tells you which ones are worth it, ranks the list against your dinner, and works in 11 languages with no account and no subscription.

Other good options, depending on what you want:

  • Price checks only, against a price database: Noir.
  • Community star ratings for every wine: Vivino (list scanning needs Premium).
  • Two or three quick picks for your meal: Tastello, Sommo or Cellar Wine Scanner.
  • The live shop price of one bottle: Wine-Searcher, which doesn't scan lists.

The apps

PocSomm

Our pick: the best all-rounder for value, food pairing and travel.

Take one photo of the wine list, or import a PDF or a link, and PocSomm gives every bottle an estimated local shop price, its markup and a clear verdict: great value, fair price or high markup. Sort the whole list by Best Value to see the best deals first, or filter it to reds, whites, rosé or sparkling. Choosing for the table? Photograph the food menu, pick everyone's dishes, and PocSomm ranks the entire wine list by how well each bottle pairs, with the reasoning in plain language. It also reads bottle labels, suggests wines for a recipe link and answers your wine questions.

Why diners choose it: no account, no ads and no tracking. No subscription either: 5 free credits, then one-time packs of 20 credits for $4.99, 60 for $9.99 or 150 for $19.99, and a list costs a credit or two. It works in 11 languages and 21 currency markets, so a list abroad gets prices for the market you're in.

Good to know: prices are AI estimates rather than live shop listings, so for the bottle you settle on a quick check is worth it. iPhone today; Android is on the way.

Noir: Wine List Scanner

Best for price-focused diners in the US who want hard benchmarks.

Noir reads a whole list, up to 100 pages, and prints a markup multiple next to each wine with a verdict: near retail is "Great value", 2–3× is "Typical", beyond that is "Steep". Its benchmarks come from a database of retail listings and auction results normalized to a 750 ml bottle, which Noir itself calls informational estimates. You can also browse lists from restaurants it already tracks, before you book. It filters by price band and accepts PDFs and spreadsheets.

Watch out for: English only, iPhone only, prices only (no tasting notes or food pairing), and an email account for scans. Three full analyses are free, then Noir Pro is $11.99 a month or a Day Pass $2.99 for 24 hours. No App Store ratings yet.

CellarTracker

Best if you already use CellarTracker, or want community valuation data.

CellarTracker added restaurant list scanning in October 2026, still labeled beta. It gives personal picks, community reviews and a markup multiple against CellarTracker's average value, compared with the typical markup at the same price level. Its community data is large: 13.6 million tasting notes, by its own count.

Watch out for: it's a beta, and CellarTracker doesn't say whether list scanning is free or needs a subscription. A free account is required.

Vivino

Best for community ratings and buying wine afterwards.

Vivino has the biggest wine community (about 70 million users, by its own count) and a marketplace of more than 500 merchants. Its wine list scanner shows the community star rating for every wine on a list. Vivino's own October 2026 guide calls it a Premium-only feature: $4.99 a month or $47.90 a year in the US. Android, iPhone and web.

Watch out for: it shows ratings, not whether the restaurant's price is fair; you compare the price yourself. The free tier shows ads, Premium needs an account, and Vivino's App Store privacy label lists Identifiers and Usage Data under "Data Used to Track You".

Sommo

Best for one subscription across iPhone, Android and the web.

Sommo's Score Menu gives every wine on a list a score that blends quality, value at the listed price and fit with the restaurant's food; a free mode gives picks with reasons. It also does label and shelf scans, a cellar, a journal and WSET study, on iPhone, iPad, Android and the web.

Watch out for: Score Menu is Pro only ($4.99 a month or $29.99 a year after 5 free lifetime scans), and it shows a blended score rather than a shop price or markup. Free account required.

Vinea AI Wine Sommelier

Best for planning ahead: it predicts a restaurant's list from its name.

Vinea parses every bottle on a list, surfaces best-value picks for your taste and flags hidden gems against overpriced bottles. With no list in hand, it predicts what a restaurant likely offers from its name. It also has a cellar and social features.

Watch out for: pricing has several layers: Premium $7.99 a month or $59.99 a year, Pro $14.99 a month or $119.99 a year, and scan-token packs from $2.99. It needs an account, and its App Store privacy label lists Identifiers under "Data Used to Track You".

Tastello: Wine List Sommelier

Best for a quick shortlist at a restaurant it already knows.

Tastello gives three bottles matched to your dish and mood, each with a short note on why, and lets you ask follow-ups such as "cheaper" or "bolder". Many venues' lists are pre-loaded, so you may not need a photo, and it can pick for a whole table.

Watch out for: three picks rather than the whole list, English only, iOS 26 or later, and Sign in with Apple is required. Core features are free; Tastello Plus is $4.99 a month or $39.99 a year. It launched publicly in November 2026.

Cellar Wine Scanner

Best free option.

Cellar reads up to 20 pages and gives picks in Entry, Mid and Splurge tiers with a short reason each, plus a Skip list of overpriced bottles. It's free, on iPhone and the web, where your first scan needs no account.

Watch out for: picks rather than a price for every wine, no markup number shown, and Sign in with Apple on iPhone.

Wine-Searcher

Best for the real price of a specific bottle.

Wine-Searcher doesn't read restaurant lists, but nothing beats it for checking what a bottle actually costs: it lists about 8 million offers from 55,000 merchants (its own figures), with price history and critic scores. Use it to double-check the one bottle you're about to order.

Watch out for: one wine at a time, so you work out the markup yourself.

Other apps we looked at

Several newer apps also read restaurant lists, each with few or no ratings so far: Which Wine?, WinePick, Wine List: Order confidently, Wine Find, Sip Savvy, Goblet, Green Glass, Vinster, GrapeGuru, VinoLens and Pocket Sommelier. Delectable doesn't scan lists, and its iPhone app was last updated in September 2021.

A note on accuracy

None of these apps, ours included, publishes a scan-accuracy figure that can be checked. Every app's prices are estimates or benchmarks of some kind, so treat a verdict as a guide and check the bottle you settle on if it matters. Frequently asked questions

What is the best app to scan a restaurant wine list?

For most diners, PocSomm: it prices and judges every bottle on the list, ranks the list against your dinner, and needs no account or subscription. For price checks only, Noir; for star ratings, Vivino (Premium); for a few quick picks, Tastello, Sommo or Cellar; for the live price of one bottle, Wine-Searcher.

Can Vivino scan a restaurant wine list for free?

No. Vivino's own October 2026 guide says the wine list scanner is Premium only: $4.99 a month or $47.90 a year in the US.

Does Wine-Searcher scan wine lists?

No. It scans labels and QR codes and searches one wine at a time. Its strength is real, current shop prices.

Which wine list apps don't need an account?

PocSomm needs none. Among the others, Cellar's web version allows a first scan without one, Noir lets you browse restaurant lists without signing in, and Which Wine? says no account is required. Most others need a free account or Sign in with Apple.
